Over Greenland

I just want to share cool things I saw from the plane.
Published

September 24, 2026

Coming back from a month in Vienna, I flew over Iceland, Greenland, and the northeast coast of Canada, and I got to see some pretty cool things.

Here is what I saw, relatively in order.

A huge ice sheet, which I thought was a flat cloud at first. Greenland appears all white on maps, because it actually is.

Land helps with seeing that it is really an ice sheet and not a flat cloud. (The blue tint is because the windows were slowly losing their tint.)

The next three images show cool new details appearing: rugged rock and an isolated rocky island in the middle of the ice.

Mountains with cool shadows. And look at that hill in the left corner!

A small piece of rock protruding from the ice. Makes me want to go and see how big it actually is.

In a wider view, we can see water channels of different colors: icy blue (maybe there is ice in there) and deeper blue. The deeper blue ones appear disconnected from the ice, so maybe lake v sea color?

The boundaries between huge ice sheets and the sea are very cool. It’s pretty much a glacier, but in the sea. Here are a few images of that.

The ice sheets extending inland in flowing shapes are neat because, if you look closely, the ice has a lot of surface structure. I think what we are seeing are marks from the downward flow of the ice. As it moves slowly, pieces break and re-merge, making lines appear parrallel to flow. Interestingly, the curves we see on the ice are a lot smoother than the curves along the edges of the ice river shores. Maybe that’s also because it’s behaving somewhat like a fluid?

We see more of these marks in the next image, but we also see very clearly the two colors that water can take. Basically, the color of lakes versus the sea, I think. We also see more of the topography thanks to the shadows (not to be confused with the lakes). Another cool feature, is that are two very straight parralle lines, next to each other. Looks like a fault line? Or a Dyke? They appear in a few of the pictures.

Tiny melted area in the huge ice flow. No idea why it melted, but we can see a valley leading to it.

Leaving the ice behind a bit, we can start noticing cliffs in the distance! A massive slab wall for the brave. And zooming in, we also see a minuscule beach (I think).

So many lakes.

The landscape looks a lot steeper in the distance.

I can now say that I have seen an iceberg.

Many icebergs.

This particular valley seems very cool. It looks deep, and somehow like it’s curving.

Now for some water things. Water coming from two distinct valleys/ice rivers is merging, and the two streams somehow have different colors. Why is there more mud in one of them??

Pictures of the coast. It would be difficult to say what altitude these were taken from with no context. Feels like it could 100 km or 5 km. Commercial flights are typically at 10 km, apparently. I think that difficulty in figuring out the distance is something fractals share!

Super far away. I feel like that distant ice flow is probably close to 100 km away from me. I dont belive all these icebergs being exported to the coast of Canada are reflected in the GDP figures of Danemark.

The farther I look, the taller the cliffs look. There is also a small mountain sticking out of the ice.

Tiny islands. But also, look at the color gradient in the water! Fresh water doing something weird to seawater? Minerals being ground down by the ice and washed down to the sea? It’s really surprising how visible it is.

Finishing up with a wide view and cool clouds.

Actually, finishing up with a circular double rainbow I saw just past Greenland. Zoom in; it’s not that small.

And some clouds with a super neat texture.

And a jet with a trail that somehow looks black.

Final thoughts: look out the window more, and save some money to go spend a month in Greenland.

And why didn’t I see these things on the previous 10 flights? Turns out it makes sense: I need a window seat (2/9), a daytime flight (~1/2), good weather (???), and to not be busy watching a movie (~1/2). So, less than a 5% chance.
